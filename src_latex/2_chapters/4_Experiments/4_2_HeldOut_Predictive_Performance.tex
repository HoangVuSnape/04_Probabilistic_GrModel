\subsection{Analytical Discussion of Predictive Outcomes}

The empirical findings from Table \ref{tab:table_a_prediction} provide decisive answers to our foundational modeling hypotheses:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Continuous Neural CPD Outperforms Tabular CPTs:}  
    Across every single cross-validation split, $H_1$ consistently outperforms $B_0$. In the pooled 5-fold evaluation, $H_1$ achieves a superior AUROC of $\mathbf{0.8082}$ (versus $0.8001$ for $B_0$, a statistically significant gain of $\Delta\text{AUROC} = +0.0081$, paired $t$-test $p = 0.014$) and improves Macro F1 from $0.7308$ to $\mathbf{0.7374}$ ($\Delta\text{F1} = +0.0067$). Crucially, $H_1$ systematically reduces the Brier Score from $0.1701$ down to $\mathbf{0.1648}$, proving that deep neural parameterization yields better-calibrated probability forecasts by effectively interpolating across sparsely populated covariate conditioning cells.
    
    \item \textbf{Failure of Discrete Inference on Sparsely Discovered Graphs ($H_2$):}  
    When continuous structure learning is combined with discrete tabular parameters without neural smoothing ($H_2$), performance collapses severely (AUROC drops to $0.6428$ and Recall plunges to $0.3795$). Because continuous discovery under high $\ell_1$-penalization recovers a sparse skeleton (1--2 active edges), discrete CPTs cannot share statistical strength across nodes, leading to severe information starvation.
    
    \item \textbf{Neural Parameterization Rescues Learned Structures ($H_3$):}  
    When the fully neural hybrid architecture ($H_3$) is deployed over the data-driven discovered graph, predictive power is completely restored (AUROC $\mathbf{0.8048}$, Brier score $\mathbf{0.1667}$), confirming that neural parameterization compensates for empirical structural sparsity through dense continuous latent feature representations.
\end{enumerate}
